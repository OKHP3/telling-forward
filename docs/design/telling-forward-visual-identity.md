# Telling Forward Visual Identity

**Status:** Working visual-language guide

**Audience:** Telling Forward designers, illustrators, product builders, and future creative collaborators

**Parent family:** OverKill Hill P3
**Applies to:** app and pinned icons, social images, repository and Replit imagery, backgrounds, small interface motifs, and future derivative artwork

## 1. The premise: narrative matter in motion

Telling Forward is not a word processor wearing a story-themed skin. It is an experiment in a different kind of authorship. Ideas can be captured before they become prose. Concepts can mature into scenes. Mature material can be deliberately disrupted or inverted to create useful new material. Paths can become canon, remain private, or become alternate continuities.

The visual identity must make that premise felt before a person reads an explanation. It should convey that narrative is alive, structured, and transformable.

The governing image is **narrative matter in motion**.

- A word, memory, character impulse, or plot possibility can be a particle.
- A capsule can be a nucleus: small, dense, and full of potential.
- Relationships can be bonds, threads, or orbits.
- A scene, chapter, or story path can be a molecule or a larger living structure.
- Maturation is fusion: separate material gathers into a more coherent form.
- Disruption and inversion are fission: a coherent form opens and releases new usable material.
- A new path is recombination, not debris.

This is an important distinction. Telling Forward does not fracture stories for spectacle, damage, or nihilism. It opens them to reveal possibility, then makes something new from what becomes available. The system should look generative, deliberate, and hopeful.

## 2. The emotional posture

The visual world is contemporary, spacious, precise, and warm. It should have the confidence of an advanced creative instrument without looking like enterprise software, a generic graph database, or a science-fiction control room. It should also avoid the expected symbols of traditional writing: books, parchment, typewriters, pens, quills, dusty archives, and fantasy props.

The desired feeling is this:

> An idea has opened. Its inner structure is visible. It can now become more than it was.

The work may be intricate, but it is never visually anxious. Complexity is organized through negative space, fine bonds, clear centers of gravity, and a limited number of meaningful accents. The reader or contributor should feel invited into a field of possibility, not trapped inside a dense diagram.

## 3. Relationship to OverKill Hill

Telling Forward belongs to the OverKill Hill P3 family, but it must not borrow the parent brand wholesale. OverKill Hill's declared profile brings forge-like technical confidence, durable material, dark structure, deliberate geometry, and a heat-tempered palette. Telling Forward translates those qualities into a more fluid and human visual language.

| OverKill Hill cue | Telling Forward translation |
| --- | --- |
| Forge-like confidence | A sense that transformation is intentional and capable |
| Schematic detail | Fine path lines, bonds, orbits, and connection logic |
| Durable material | Mineral, fiber, translucent, and lightly faceted texture |
| Orange and amber heat | Energy released when material connects, changes, or resolves |
| Dark technical surface | A supporting contrast color, not the dominant field |

The result should feel related through precision, warmth, and conviction. It should not look like a workshop, blueprint, machine room, or industrial dashboard.

## 4. The light-first palette

Telling Forward is light-first. Its principal canvas is the OverKill Hill paper tone, `#F6F2EE`. This is not a retreat into nostalgia. It is a generous field where structures, paths, and creative material can breathe.

Dark colors define and ground. Warm colors mark transformation. No color should be used merely to decorate a screen.

| Role | Approved family color | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary field | Paper `#F6F2EE` | Main canvas, social-image space, reading-adjacent background |
| Quiet structure | Teal `#1C3A34` | Bonds, deeper clusters, selected icon detail |
| Grounding contrast | Espresso `#2A2320` | Small text, key outlines, rare deep surfaces |
| Material depth | Rust `#5B3A27` | Facets, mature elements, selected emphasis |
| Active transformation | Orange `#C46A2C` | Path activation, a meaningful fork, an author action |
| Resolution and release | Amber `#E6A03C` | Convergence, connection, acceptance, generative change |

Use translucent layers, tint, texture, and spacing to create range before introducing more colors. Dark surfaces should appear as focal contrast, not as a default background. Orange and amber should stay scarce enough to retain meaning.

### Color semantics

- **Paper** is possibility and openness.
- **Teal** is relationship, continuity, and quiet intelligence.
- **Espresso** is structure and legibility.
- **Rust** is depth, history, and materiality.
- **Orange** is an active transformation.
- **Amber** is a meaningful moment of connection, release, or resolution.

Do not use color alone to communicate editorial, privacy, or canon state. Those states require clear labels, shape differences, and accessible text in addition to any color treatment.

## 5. The visual grammar

The system has a small vocabulary. Reuse it consistently so new derivatives remain members of the same family.

### The nucleus

A nucleus is a compact center of potential. It can represent a capsule, a story seed, a character persona, a scene center, or a cluster of related thoughts. It may be circular, faceted, or softly irregular, but it is never a generic avatar or card icon.

### The bond

A bond is a fine line between meaningful units. Bonds may arc, thread, orbit, or run in a measured path. They should not form a dense, literal database visualization. A bond tells the eye that two things have a relationship worth following.

### The strand

A strand is a longer relationship across material. It can suggest a prose sequence, a plot line, a contributor's development, or a story path. Strands may weave, split, and reconverge. They should remain elegant and spacious, more like an intentional current than a technical wiring harness.

### The fracture

A fracture is an opening into possibility. It can be a seam, a split edge, a branching line, or a field of released fragments. It should never read as violence, failure, or a broken interface. Fractures open rather than shatter.

### The gold connection

Amber or gold-toned detail appears at a generative transition: a bond made, a path accepted, an insight released, or a new structure formed. This is the visual echo of repair and recombination. It is not ornamental gold leaf and should not be used as a generic premium effect.

### The fragment

A fragment is a small reusable unit. It may be an irregular faceted particle, a quiet chip of color, or a tiny cluster. Fragments should look recoverable and capable of joining something new. Avoid visual debris.

## 6. The two complementary image directions

The identity supports two related art directions. They share the same palette, grammar, and purpose, but have different jobs.

### Narrative Chemistry

This direction is active, connective, and kinetic. It uses nuclei, orbits, bonds, and flowing strands to show ideas combining. It is appropriate for:

- Concept Board accents and empty states
- small interactive motifs and motion
- an icon family based on orbit, bond, or junction shapes
- social images about contribution, connection, and shared creation

Narrative Chemistry must remain poetic. It should evoke relationships between living ideas, not a classroom atomic diagram or a technical node editor.

### Recombinant Story Matter

This direction is quieter and more editorial. It shows a formed structure opening into fragments, then gathering into a newly coherent shape. It is appropriate for:

- launch and share imagery
- reader backgrounds and storyworld entry moments
- editorial campaigns and long-form presentations
- illustration for maturation, disruption, inversion, and alternate paths

This is the strongest direction for explaining that disruption is productive. A released fragment field should feel like potential becoming visible, with light and gold connection energy indicating a new future rather than damage.

## 7. Applying the system across surfaces

### App and pinned icons

The icon needs one memorable silhouette. Favor a simple three-way junction, a single opening seam, or a compact bond-and-nucleus mark. The mark must survive small sizes and must not depend on texture, fine type, or a complex scene.

Create a small state family by varying the same core mark, not by inventing new logos:

- default: paper field with teal or espresso structure and one amber point
- active: a single orange or amber bond becomes prominent
- dark support variant: paper or amber structure on espresso

### Social, GitHub, and Replit imagery

Social images should be cinematic but spacious. Use one dominant transformation: a strand entering a field of particles, a cluster reconverging, or a bright connection crossing a large light field. Preserve generous clear space for any required title, account name, or platform metadata.

Repository and Replit imagery can be slightly more structural. A cropped hero image plus the icon mark is usually sufficient. Avoid trying to describe the whole platform in one thumbnail.

### Backgrounds and screensavers

Backgrounds should be quiet enough to live behind text. Use subtle contour flow, thin bonds, faint particle clusters, and rare amber connection points. They must never compete with reading or make a contributor feel that every surface is a busy visualization.

### Interface motifs and widgets

Small marks can make the app feel native to the identity without turning every control into a metaphor.

| Product meaning | Suggested visual treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Capsule or concept | Nucleus or compact fragment |
| Related material | Fine bond or continuity thread |
| Story path | Graceful strand with distinct directionality |
| New possibility | Small released fragment cluster |
| Maturation | Several units drawing into a more coherent form |
| Disrupt or invert | A formed unit opening into purposeful new fragments |
| Accepted connection | A restrained amber junction or seam |

The application still needs conventional clarity. Use familiar controls, direct names, visible state, keyboard focus, and readable labels. The visual grammar enriches meaning; it never replaces usability.

## 8. Typography, texture, and motion

Use the OverKill Hill family roles with restraint:

- **Alfa Slab One** is reserved for decisive display moments, a campaign title, or a rare brand statement. It is not body copy and should not make the product feel retro.
- **DM Sans** is the working voice for reading, navigation, forms, and human-facing prose.
- **JetBrains Mono** is for small structured details where it helps a person understand a relationship, reference, or system fact. It must remain backstage and sparing.

Texture should be tactile but subtle: pale mineral grain, translucent fibers, soft faceted material, and thin contour lines. Avoid heavy paper distress, photorealistic stone, fake gold foil, or visual noise.

Motion expresses transformation. A bond may draw into place, a cluster may ease together, and a fracture may open to reveal fragments. Movement should be brief, measured, and optional. Respect reduced-motion preferences and never hide an important state change inside animation.

## 9. Derivative-making rules

Before creating a new icon, illustration, social card, background, or widget motif, ask:

1. Does it show matter connecting, opening, or recombining?
2. Does it use light space as the primary field?
3. Is amber or orange communicating a meaningful transformation rather than filling space?
4. Does it preserve one of the established forms: nucleus, bond, strand, fracture, gold connection, or fragment?
5. Could it still be recognized as Telling Forward at a small size or when cropped?
6. Does it avoid both generic software imagery and traditional writing nostalgia?

If the answer to several questions is no, it may be attractive but it is not yet part of this family.

## 10. Guardrails and accessibility

Do not use:

- literal books, pages, pens, typewriters, parchment, or library imagery;
- literal forges, gears, blueprints, workshop scenes, or industrial decoration;
- generic neon cyberpunk, dark-mode-only framing, or corporate gradients;
- dense graph diagrams, academic atom symbols, or destructive explosion imagery;
- breakage presented as loss, violence, or failure;
- texture or animated detail that reduces text legibility.

Every derivative must preserve sufficient text contrast, clear focus treatment, visible state beyond color, text alternatives for meaningful imagery, and a reduced-motion path. The image language should make Telling Forward more understandable and more human, never less accessible.

## 11. Working conclusion

Telling Forward presents authorship as a living transformation system. Material can cohere, open, release, and become coherent again. The visual identity gives that idea a repeatable form: a bright field, structured relationships, warm transformation energy, and visible recombination.

The lasting family resemblance is not one illustration style. It is the conviction that a story can be opened without being ruined, and remade without losing the trace of how it came to be.
