# Content and story rights

Unless a file or contribution says otherwise, fiction, storyworld material, character material, personal testimony, prompts, and other creative content in this repository are **all rights reserved** by their respective creators.

The public availability of this repository does not grant permission to copy, adapt, publish, train on, or create derivative works from that content. Please preserve attribution and ask before reusing anything that is not explicitly licensed.

Platform source code may receive a separate open-source license in a later change. Until that boundary is documented in a dedicated license file, do not assume that repository content is open source.

Contributors are responsible for submitting only material they created or are authorized to share. A future contribution agreement will define permissions for material accepted into shared worlds, proposed canon, and alternate paths.

## Proposed rights architecture (not yet in force)

The concept under exploration is a **stewarded commons**, not automatic platform ownership. A contributor would ordinarily retain copyright in the original expression they add. For an explicitly open world, a separate contribution agreement could grant the world steward and the platform the rights needed to host, preserve, display, adapt, and authorize compatible continuations, while keeping attribution and lineage visible.

The originating author would retain the rights they reserve in the seed and canon. A later contributor would retain the rights they reserve in their new material. A derivative branch therefore does not become ownerless: it contains preexisting material plus new, attributable expression. Any commercial adaptation would need a documented chain of rights and would use only the contributions authorized and selected under the applicable agreement.

The project may eventually test a noncommercial pilot, a share-alike world license, or a custom commercial contribution agreement. Those are different choices and must not be presented as interchangeable. No contributor should be asked to surrender rights through an unclear click-through or an implied “community ownership” promise.

Virtual credits, if ever introduced, would be a product-design experiment rather than proof of ownership or investment. They should not replace a written agreement governing real commercial payments, adaptation rights, attribution, or contributor eligibility.

This document is a project policy, not legal advice.
